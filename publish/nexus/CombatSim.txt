DESCRIPTION (paste as is: it is in Nexus formatting)

[size=5][b]Combat Simulator[/b][/size]
What does an hour of this fight give me, what does it cost, and will I die? This answers it without fighting.

It does [b]not[/b] re-create the game's combat formulas. It loads a copy of your character into a second, hidden game and runs the game's own combat on it, in the background. An hour of fighting takes a fraction of a second. Nothing the copy does is ever saved.

[size=4][b]A first simulation[/b][/size]
[list=1]
[*]Press the round button with the chart, in the strip of round buttons on the right edge of the screen.
[*][b]Where[/b]: tick an area (or several, or none for everything you have unlocked).
[*][b]Each fight lasts[/b]: 1 hour is a good start. That is game time, not waiting time.
[*]Press [b]Simulate[/b]. The lines appear as they finish; click one for its details.
[/list]
The button beside the red X folds the window down to its title so you can follow a scan while you play. Click it again or press the round chart button to bring the results back.

[size=4][b]What you get, per hour[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]Kills, XP, Gold, Food, Food per kill, Deaths, Low HP[/b] for every fight, in a table you sort by clicking a column. The first click puts the best fights on top: the most XP, but the fewest deaths. A small arrow after the column's name shows which way it is sorted.
[*][b]Columns[/b]: choose which numbers the table shows and in which order, out of 43 in groups: runes, ammo and prayer points used, what the supplies cost and the gold left after them, damage taken, Safe or Risky, the enemy's biggest hit, the XP of each skill, dungeon runs and more. Presets set several at once (Idle, Money, Supplies, Training, Dungeons). The window keeps its width: when the numbers need more room, they move sideways, a column at a time, under the enemy's name.
[*][b]Details[/b]: XP per skill, loot item by item with its value, what was used (food, runes, arrows, prayer points), damage taken, when the first death came, food and healing per kill, how many kills the food in your bank is enough for.
[*][b]How sure[/b]: every rate comes with its 95% range, which holds the true number 95 times in 100. Put the pointer on a line for the ranges of its numbers ("Kills ± 7.9%", "Deaths up to 3.69", because no deaths in an hour is not "never"); in the details each rate has its range under it. A rare drop that did not come in the time simulated is in no range: when an enemy's gold hangs on one, simulate it for 12 hours.
[*][b]Compared with[/b]: the same fight with other equipment or another spell, side by side, for every column of the table, mostly in percent: green where this one is better, red where it is worse, but only when the two ranges do not overlap. Otherwise it is grey and says "could be luck". XP in a skill one setup does not train at all, like Magic with a sword, is a real 0: any XP in it is better. The columns in which both are the same share one line.
[*][b]On the enemy cards[/b] of the game's combat areas menu: [b]Safe[/b] or [b]Risky[/b], XP and deaths per hour, from your newest scan of that enemy made with what you have on now. Safe means no deaths, and no hit of that enemy reaches your auto-eat point. A grey "Other setup" tag says the scan was made with other equipment. Click a tag to open its scan; opening the menu never simulates anything.
[/list]

[size=4][b]What you can change[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]Equipment[/b]: pick one of your saved loadouts, or click any slot to try another item: every item of the game that fits, with a search box, whether you own it or not. A two-handed weapon fills both hands, as on the game's equipment screen; if the copy ever wears something other than the slots show, the result says so.
[*][b]Spells[/b]: when the weapon casts spells, choose which one to fight with.
[*][b]Compare[/b]: tick it for two setups, A and B (a saved loadout, or one with an item or the spell changed), and press Simulate once: every fight runs with both, from the same moment of your character. Each column of the table becomes three: under its name which setup is better and by how much ("A by 23%", "A ×4"), then the number with A and with B, green and red only when the two ranges do not overlap, grey where it could be luck. Click a column's name for the fights where the choice matters most; a line above the table counts the fights each setup wins, and in Best for says where each setup does best. Details shows the fight with both setups side by side, and Fight is for the setup shown on the left.
[*][b]Supplies[/b]: Unlimited gives the rates of a fight that is kept supplied; From bank uses what you own and tells you what ran out, and when.
[*][b]Go on until[/b]: each fight goes on after its time until every number in the table is known to the size you choose ("± 1% sure" to "± 10% sure"), 12 hours of fighting at most. A number that 12 hours cannot bring that far goes on until it is within the next larger size it can reach; one that cannot reach even ± 10%, like a few deaths or gold that hangs on a rare drop, does not hold it back. A smaller size never ends a fight sooner than a larger one. Not with supplies From bank.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Best for[/b][/size]
Not "what does this area give" but "where should I go". Choose the skill you want to train and where to look: every fight is tried in each attack stance of your weapon, and the fights are ranked by what they give that skill, with the best stance named on each line.

[size=4][b]History and Fight[/b][/size]
The last ten scans of a character are kept, also after the game was closed: look at one again without simulating anything. The red [b]Fight[/b] button starts the selected fight in the game.

[b]Wear this setup first[/b], the tick box above the Fight button, is off until you tick it. Ticked, Fight first puts on what the result was simulated with, through the game's own actions, then starts the fight:
[list]
[*]Another saved loadout: it changes to it, as the game's loadout buttons do, with the food, prayers, attack stance and spells the game keeps in it.
[*]An item you swapped in: it puts it on from your bank, and what it replaces goes back to the bank. Ammunition: as many as your bank holds, never more.
[*]A spell you chose: it selects it.
[*]An item that is not in your bank, that you cannot wear yet, or whose old piece your bank has no room for: it is skipped, and that slot stays exactly as it is.
[*]A slot you emptied: what you wear there stays on. Fight never just takes a piece off.
[/list]
The text above the button names every step before you press it, or why nothing goes on, and Fight never does more than it says. If your equipment or bank changed since the text was written, nothing is put on and no fight starts. When everything went on, the window closes; when a slot stayed as it was or a piece could not go on as the text said, the window stays open and says what really happened. When the game does not do a step as it should, Fight stops there and starts no fight. Nothing is bought, made or put back. When the box puts nothing on for a fight, it looks greyed: point at it for why.

[size=4][b]Good to know[/b][/size]
[list]
[*]Fights are random, but the dice of each fight are set from the fight and the setup: the same setup and the same character give the same numbers again. The copy is your character as it is when you press Simulate, XP, slayer task and potions included; while you play these move on, so a scan a minute later can differ a little, and more for the enemy of your slayer task. The ranges say how far luck could have moved the numbers; longer fights narrow them.
[*]An item whose requirements you do not meet cannot be equipped by the copy either; the result says so.
[*]Up to four fights are simulated side by side. If the game stutters during a scan, set "Workers" in mods\CombatSim\settings.json to 1 or 2.
[*]The Fight button changes your equipment only with [b]Wear this setup first[/b] ticked.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Install[/b][/size]
[list=1]
[*]Install the [url=https://www.nexusmods.com/melvoridle2/mods/2]Melvor Mod Loader[/url] (0.1.3 or newer).
[*]Extract this zip into the game folder: Steam > right-click Melvor Idle 2 > Manage > Browse local files. The file ends up as [font=Courier New]mods\CombatSim\CombatSim.dll[/font].
[*]Start the game.
[/list]
To update, extract the new zip over the old one.

[size=4][b]Uninstall[/b][/size]
Delete the folder [font=Courier New]mods\CombatSim[/font]. Its settings and earlier scans are in that folder. The mod never writes your save: only Fight with Wear this setup first ticked changes your equipment, through the game's own actions.

[size=4][b]About[/b][/size]
Made for game version 0.11.6-alpha.1 with Melvor Mod Loader 0.1.3. The game is in early access: an update can break the mod until it is rebuilt. It then switches itself off and says so in MelvorModLoader.log; the game and your other mods keep running.
It uses the game's own textures, fonts and colours, so it looks like part of the game. It gives no items, XP or bonuses.
